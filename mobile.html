Chapter 1 — your plan

In automatic mode YOU are the director: the plan is yours, in your own words — nothing here is invented for you. The engine stages it into scenes, writes them, checks them for continuity and keeps memory. The protagonist is fully automatic. When the chapter is written, you write the next chapter's plan.

Each part is one call to the story model. Both can be changed any time from the story page. End a substoryline with a number in brackets, like (12), to set its parts yourself.

The style and guidelines below are re-sent with every single request, so the storyteller never drifts from them.

The Novel

Director

The author's desk — plans laid ahead of play: the book's spine → the open chapter's substorylines → the offers each substory opens once you step into it. Everything is editable; the storyteller treats it all as a menu, never a script. The Director can be turned off in Settings.

Chapters

Scene prep

The book premise · throughline · tone

Threads the world's plans in motion

One per line: Title | agenda | next step | due/trigger — maintained by the memory keeper, fired by the storyteller.

Campaign notes yours — whispers direction to the director

Memory

The memory keeper updates this automatically after each scene. Everything here is editable — it's what the storyteller "remembers". Changes save instantly.

Current setting

A rolling snapshot of the scene right now — rewritten by the memory keeper each scene so the storyteller never loses track of where everyone is.

On stage

The actors physically present right now — surface reads only (hidden agendas live in hidden rolls and in each person's card). One per line: Name | look | doing | wants

In this conversation 💬 agendas

When you open a 💬 talk, the conversation director reads the room once: what each person present wants out of this talk, avoids, how they speak, where they steer. Refreshed when the cast changes.

Everyone the story knows — tap a person to open their card: how they look, what they are to you, the history you share, and the intentions they hide. Memories and intentions are living: the keeper updates them, moves a hidden intention out once it is resolved or openly admitted.

The places of the story, as canon.

Events and conversations, merged in story order — the spine of what has actually happened.

World state

The clock of the world — the memory keeper advances it after each scene, and the storyteller must keep it moving. Fully editable.

World notes

The codex of the world — what the story has established about customs, law, faith, places, history, how things work. It grows as the knowledge of the world develops.

How you play

Switching replaces the storyteller prompt with the style's template and changes the input boxes on the story page. The story so far is kept.

Automatic novel

Used when the story is an automatic novel. The scene planner is the Director model; the novelist is Story model 1; the continuity editor is set under Models. Scene length follows the prose level (direct · mid · rich).

A substoryline ending in a number in brackets, like (12), gets exactly that many. Words per part follow the prose level.

Scene painting & narration

Image model

Uses your OpenRouter key. Only models that can OUTPUT images work here (e.g. google/gemini-2.5-flash-image, sometimes listed with a -preview suffix). Images are kept in this browser (they're too large for save files).

Prompt templates

The standardized structures sent to each model. Placeholders like {STYLE}, {GUIDELINES}, {LORE}, {MEMORY}, {LANGUAGE}, {PROTAGONIST} are filled in automatically. Edit freely; "Reset" restores defaults.
